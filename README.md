Project Overview:Developed a thin-film gas sensor for electronic nose (E-nose) applications. To resolve the critical challenge of cross-sensitivity and low selectivity in conventional metal-oxide sensors, this project focuses on novel material structures—specifically integrating a physical filter membrane—rather than relying on machine learning algorithms.   
Technologies Used:Materials: SnO2 thin-film sensing base modified with Pt nanoparticles, coupled with a ZnO surface filter layer.  
Fabrication: DC Magnetron Sputtering to precisely control the ZnO filter thickness at 5nm, 10nm, and 30nm.   
Testing & Measurement: Designed and operated a dynamic gas measurement system using Mass Flow Controllers (MFCs) to analyze sensor responses to H2, NH3, and Ethanol at concentrations ranging from 100 to 300 ppm.   
Key Achievements:Blocking Filter Effect: Demonstrated that a 5nm ZnO layer acts as a spatial barrier, successfully blocking small inorganic gases (H2, NH3) from penetrating the SnO2/Pt layer, which significantly suppressed cross-sensitivity.   Intermediate Catalytic Effect: Discovered that a 10nm ZnO layer acts as a catalyst, converting Ethanol into highly reactive intermediates. This amplified the sensor's response by over 3.2 times at 250°C compared to the baseline sensor.   Impact:Successfully generated a multi-dimensional "Radar Fingerprint" dataset with distinct morphological signatures for each target gas.   Proved the efficacy of physical filter layers in enhancing the gas classification performance of E-nose systems, demonstrating high potential for smart environmental monitoring and non-invasive medical breath analysis.
